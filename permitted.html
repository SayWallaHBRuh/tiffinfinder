How to get permitted in Calgary

A step-by-step guide for tiffin kitchens — home kitchens, restaurants, caterers and rented commercial kitchens — checked against official Alberta Health Services, Government of Alberta and City of Calgary pages on 23 Sep 2026. Rules change and every kitchen is different, so confirm each step with AHS and the City before you spend money.

The short version

AHS says commercial food businesses, including caterers and home-based businesses that prepare high-risk foods, need an AHS Food Handling Permit, issued after an approval inspection. Caterers need the permit for an approved kitchen where they prepare their food. Alberta’s Food Regulation exempts only low-risk home-prepared food, so a home kitchen making high-risk food, or food containing meat, poultry, seafood or unpasteurized milk, still needs a permit and an approved food establishment. In Calgary you also need a City business licence, Food Service – Premises. Tiffin Finder lists only kitchens with a permit for the kitchen they cook in.

Sources: AHS, Starting a Food Business (PDF) (opens in a new tab) · Food Regulation, s.3 (PDF) (opens in a new tab) · City of Calgary, Home-based food business (opens in a new tab)

Your path to a permit

  1. 1

    Check your food against the exemption

    Alberta’s Food Regulation defines low-risk food as food that generally won’t grow harmful bacteria without temperature control. Tiffin meals that have to be kept hot or chilled most likely fall outside the home-food exemption, so ask AHS to confirm for your menu. See what it covers.

  2. 2

    Choose your route

    A permitted second kitchen at home, or renting time in a kitchen that is already permitted. Compare the two routes.

  3. 3

    Call AHS before you spend money

    AHS Environmental Public Health takes calls Monday to Friday at 1-833-476-4743. Ask what your plan needs before you build, rent or buy anything.

  4. 4

    Check the City’s location approval

    The City’s location-approval examples list preparing food in a second commercial-grade kitchen in your home as Home Occupation – Class 2, and doing paperwork in a home office while you cook in a licensed commercial kitchen as Class 1. The City says not to sign a lease until you’ve checked location approval.

  5. 5

    Second-kitchen route: plans and building permits

    Draw a scaled floor plan and submit it at least six weeks before construction; in Calgary it goes to the City’s planning department, which shares it with AHS. The second kitchen needs a commercial building permit and may need a mechanical permit, and the development permit comes first.

  6. 6

    Get a food safety certificate

    Alberta’s Food Regulation (s.31) says a commercial food establishment with 5 or fewer food handlers working must have at least one person with care and control of it who holds a food sanitation and hygiene training certificate. With 6 or more, a certified manager or supervisor must be on the premises. Certificates come from Alberta-approved food safety courses, not from AHS. The rule doesn’t apply where only unpackaged low-risk food, or food sold in the same packaging it arrived in, is offered.

  7. 7

    Apply for the Food Handling Permit

    Apply online through AHS’s Open a Business page (opens in a new tab), then get ready for the approval inspection: construction finished, equipment working, premises clean. AHS aims to book it within 7 business days of receiving your documents.

  8. 8

    Pay the permit fee

    After you pass, AHS emails an invoice for the permit fee, and emails the permit once you’ve paid it. Post it where customers can see it; it’s valid for one year, so renew it every year.

  9. 9

    Get your City business licence

    The City’s licence is Food Service – Premises, both for cooking at home and for a home office while you cook in a licensed commercial kitchen. The City needs a separate licence for each location.

  10. 10

    Get listed

    Send us a copy of your permit. See how listing works.

Sources: AHS, Starting a Food Business (PDF) (opens in a new tab) · AHS, Open a Business (opens in a new tab) · Food Regulation, s.1 and s.31 (PDF) (opens in a new tab) · City of Calgary, Home-based food business (opens in a new tab)

What the home-based exemption covers, and what it doesn’t

Covered: no permit needed under the Food Regulation

  • Low-risk foods made in your own home. The Food Regulation defines low-risk food as food that generally won’t grow harmful bacteria without temperature control; the Government of Alberta describes these as foods that don’t require refrigeration, such as baked goods, candies, whole fresh produce and some canned goods.
  • Sold directly to customers from home (including online or mail-order), at special events or at farmers’ markets.
  • Labelled with: made in a home kitchen that isn’t inspected, not for resale, your name and business name with an email or phone number, the product name, and the date it was made.

Not covered

  • Anything containing meat, poultry, seafood or unpasteurized milk (Food Regulation, s.1).
  • Food that needs temperature control, such as refrigeration, to stay safe (Food Regulation, s.1).
  • Selling or providing it to permitted food facilities (AHS), reselling it, or selling food that uses it as an ingredient (Food Regulation, s.52.1).

Because low-risk food is food that stays safe without temperature control, a cooked tiffin meal that has to be kept hot or chilled, including a vegetarian one, generally won’t qualify. If yours doesn’t, you’ll need one of the two routes below. If you’re unsure where a food lands, ask AHS at 1-833-476-4743.

The City of Calgary says all home-based food businesses that prepare or sell food need a business licence and location approval.

Two routes to a permitted kitchen

A permitted second kitchen at home

From AHS

  • Plan review of the kitchen layout (in Calgary, through the City’s planning department)
  • A certified food safety person, which the Food Regulation requires (certificates come from Alberta-approved courses, not from AHS)
  • A Food Handling Permit application
  • An approval inspection once the build is finished
  • The permit fee

From the City

  • A Home Occupation – Class 2 development permit, which the City says doesn’t guarantee an approval
  • A commercial building permit, and possibly a mechanical permit
  • The Food Service – Premises business licence

Renting time in an already-permitted kitchen

From AHS

  • Ask AHS what your arrangement needs before you sign. AHS lists a form called Permission to Use an Approved Food Establishment; ask whether it applies to you or whether you need a permit of your own.
  • Caterers need a permit for an approved kitchen where they prepare food.

From the City

  • The Food Service – Premises business licence
  • Home Occupation – Class 1, which the City’s examples give for doing paperwork in a home office while you cook in a licensed commercial kitchen
  • The City requires a separate licence for each business location, so ask which applies to you.

Fees and timelines, as listed

Only amounts an official page states. They change, so check the linked page before you budget.

AHS Food Handling Permit
Listed as $100 (Class I), $175 (Class II), $250 (Class III) or $500 (Class IV) in the Food Handling Permit fee schedule in AHS’s Starting a Food Business guide as of 23 Sep 2026. The guide says the permit is valid for one year and an annual renewal fee is invoiced. The class depends on the kind of food handling; ask AHS which class applies to your kitchen.
Source: AHS, Starting a Food Business (PDF) (opens in a new tab)

AHS aims to book it within 7 business days of receiving the required documents.
Source: AHS, Starting a Food Business (PDF) (opens in a new tab)
Plan review
Submit plans at least six weeks before construction.
Source: AHS, Starting a Food Business (PDF) (opens in a new tab)
Home Occupation – Class 2
Listed at $481 ($449 permit + $32 advertising) on the City’s page as of 23 Sep 2026. The same page gives about 10–12 weeks, and also 60 days to a decision plus a 21-day advertisement and appeal period.

Home Occupation – Class 1
Listed at $0 and about 5 business days on the City’s page as of 23 Sep 2026.

Commercial building permit
About 21 business days on the City’s page as of 23 Sep 2026; the fee comes from the City’s calculator.

Food Service – Premises licence
Listed at a $172 base fee for a new licence ($131 renewal) on the City’s 2026 fee schedule as of 23 Sep 2026. The schedule notes that its fire inspection and planning fees don’t apply to home-based businesses.

Look up a kitchen’s public inspection record

AHS posts reports online for food facility inspections completed in the past three years.

Search AHS inspection reports (opens in a new tab)

Reports list the violations an inspector noted. Safe practices aren’t listed, so a report isn’t the full picture. Reports cover the past three years, and violations usually appear within one business day (sometimes up to 5).

Households can browse the records by facility type (such as Food), city or area, and community. When a Tiffin Finder listing has a public-record link, it opens that kitchen’s record.

Tiffin Finder matches each kitchen to its record when checking the permit. A permit check is not a food-safety inspection or endorsement.

Sources: AHS, Public Health Inspection Reports (opens in a new tab) · AHS, Inspection report search (opens in a new tab)

What Tiffin Finder checks

Before a kitchen is listed we ask for a copy of its current AHS Food Handling Permit and match the kitchen to its public Alberta Health Services inspection record and, where visible, its City of Calgary business licence. When that checks out, the listing shows a badge like “Permit checked · 15 Sep 2026” with the date we checked. Until then it shows “Verification pending” and ordering stays closed. If the permit’s expiry date passes, the badge comes off and the listing shows “Permit being re-checked”.

A permit check is our check that a permit exists and is current. It is not an inspection result, a grade, or any kind of endorsement by AHS. Kitchens must tell us if their permit changes, and we remove any listing that receives an AHS closure order.
